# Studio and consultancy sites: teardown for koivupiilo.fi

Researched 2026-10-31. All pages below were fetched live with WebFetch, and homepages plus linked CSS were downloaded with curl to check font stacks, `prefers-color-scheme` and `hreflang`.

**Limits of the method.** WebFetch returns text, not pixels. Notes on typography and colour come from CSS font-family declarations and the copy, not from screenshots. "No dark mode" means no `prefers-color-scheme: dark` rule turned up in the HTML or the stylesheets I could reach. Some CSS was bundled or inlined and may have been missed.

**Not verified (not fetched):** Oak, Iconfactory, Instrument, Josh Comeau, Frank Chimero, Rachel Andrew, Jeremy Keith's personal site, Nitor and Hoodie. Set Studio's individual case-study pages could not be checked because `/work/` returned 404 and the homepage cards had no hrefs.

## 1. Per-site teardown

### CSS Wizardry: https://csswizardry.com (Harry Roberts, solo consultancy)
- **Job for the client:** proves competence straight away. The H1 states the niche ("Web Performance Optimisation, Consultancy, and Engineering"), the subhead makes a claim ("Proven results improving Core Web Vitals for world-leading organisations"), and three service cards (Audits / Consultancy / Training) sit above roughly 45 client logos and five named testimonials. The testimonial authors include the BBC iPlayer lead and Addy Osmani.
- **Mechanic worth stealing:** **one person, run as a company, with a sharp niche in the H1.** The headline names the field rather than the person. Each service gets a one-line job description ("Explains why a site is slow and how to fix it"). "Book a Free Call" sits in the hero and repeats at the end of every case study. The case studies run only about 500 words, carry one CrUX chart and a before/after table of metrics (LCP 2.8s to 2.0s, CLS 0.18 to 0.01), and openly admit the metric that barely moved: INP "simply doesn't count". That honesty is the trust signal.
- **Reject:** the contact page. It is a single email line (shown as harry@csswizardry.com, though the mailto points to Gmail) and "Or book a free call:" with no visible link in the fetched page. There is no guidance on what to send and no response-time promise. Seven service items in the nav (Masterclasses, Sentinel, Downloads and more) also blur the offer.
- **Language:** English only.
- **Dark mode:** none found.
- **Type and colour:** system-ui body, a serif stack for display (New York / Charter), Operator Mono for code. The site is mostly default type set with care, and fast by design.

### Set Studio: https://set.studio (Andy Bell's boutique studio)
- **Job:** shows fit for clients who are tired of agencies. The H1 is "Move fast (without breaking things)". Services fall into three groups: Design, Technology (which names stacks such as Astro, Eleventy, Kirby and Craft) and Strategy. The page gives outcome numbers (">70% lower conversion cost", "7x subscriptions", "six to eight figures").
- **Mechanic worth stealing:** **a two-column "Us vs. a traditional agency" table.** Rows include "Senior level staff working on your project" vs "Less senior and junior members", "Direct contact with recognised experts" vs "Access to an account manager who relays information", and "We take complete charge, allowing you to do your day job in peace" vs "Frequent requests for sign-off". It answers the main objection to hiring one person (scale) by turning it into a feature. For Jarkko the line would be "you talk to the person writing the code".
- **Reject:** the loud 3D hero lettering and decorative arrows. They suit a design studio, but on an engineering consultancy they read as costume. Case-study cards that don't link anywhere (no hrefs on the homepage) are a dead end for proof.
- **Language:** English only.
- **Dark mode:** none found. Individual case studies use their own dark or vivid themes.
- **Type and colour:** Haffer (display) and Saans (body), with bold colour blocks. Expressive and playful.

### thoughtbot: https://thoughtbot.com (the Rails studio, Jarkko's closest peer by stack)
- **Job:** proves competence in high-stakes work. The H1 is "When the stakes are high, experience matters". Services are grouped by type (Development: Rails, Hotwire, AI/ML, mobile) and by stage (validate, build, augment, fractional leadership). Industry pages cover Healthcare, Finance and Insurance.
- **Mechanic worth stealing:** **case-study cards that lead with the outcome, not the client.** Examples: "10x faster releases", "0 to 4M users in months", "Market-validated MVP in <6 weeks", "Delivery times 50% faster". The case-study page (CloseKnit Health) puts the client and the services and stack used in a header strip, then the metric, then **"The Result" before "What we did"**. Sections are headed with the client's question ("How do you create a product that can handle multiple integrations and bring it to market fast?"), and named client quotes are spread through the page.
- **Reject:** the hire form behind "Let's talk", which frames the first step as "usually takes less than 5 minutes". That is fine for a 100-person firm, but it is friction for a solo consultant, where an email gets a faster human reply. Also reject the generic claims ("over 1,000 positive outcomes").
- **Language:** English only.
- **Dark mode:** none found in the stylesheet (169 KB checked).
- **Type and colour:** heading, body and CTA font tokens, a polished corporate look with photo-heavy cards.

### Kisko Labs: https://www.kiskolabs.com (Helsinki Rails studio, the closest Finnish peer)
- **Job:** shows how an engagement works for Finnish SMEs. The H1 is "Autamme sinua löytämään oikean tien". The offers are branded by product stage: **Kisko Discovery™ (validate), Delivery™ (first version), Durability™ (fixed monthly fee, "ilman yllätyksiä"), and Pulse™.** An "investment calculator" ("Selvitä mitä ideasi voisi maksaa") lets a client estimate cost.
- **Mechanic worth stealing:** **named people as the contact point.** The CEO and the Operations Director are each listed with email, phone, LinkedIn and their own "Varaa tapaaminen" link. Case-study headlines also state the result in plain Finnish ("Excelistä omaan järjestelmään neljässä kuukaudessa"), followed by Lähtökohta and Ratkaisu sections, the scope of the work and a timeline. The cost calculator is the second idea worth taking: a rough price signal without a price list.
- **Reject:** trademarked offer names (™ on a three-word service name feels corporate for a solo consultant), and case studies with **no metrics, no quotes and no product screenshots**, only photos. B7Asunnot gives only dates.
- **Language:** Finnish first. The only English is a footer link, "Summary in English", pointing to a single page. There is no switcher and no `hreflang`. This is the weakest bilingual handling of the set, and the bar to beat for a FI/EN site.
- **Dark mode:** one `prefers-color-scheme` reference in the HTML. Not confirmed as a full dark theme.
- **Type and colour:** Geist and Phosphor icons on Webflow. Clean, but generic SaaS.

### Clearleft: https://clearleft.com (Brighton design consultancy, Jeremy Keith's studio)
- **Job:** shows fit as a partner. The H1 is "Your strategic design partner". Proof comes from three featured case studies, a podcast, and the Leading Design conference.
- **Mechanic worth stealing:** **the main CTA is an email with a person attached.** It reads "Email us about your design challenge" (mailto info@), with a second link: "Email Rich about your design challenge". The contact page offers email, phone, a HubSpot calendar for the cofounder, and walking directions from Brighton station. The Virgin Holidays case study opens with a **Results block of three headline outcomes** ("Record business growth on the web", "Sustainable in-house design capability", "A renewed focus on digital evolution"), then "The Full Story" under question headings, a quote from the lead, the engagement length (18 months) and a closing "You're unique: Email Rich".
- **Reject:** soft metrics ("8% over 2 years", "faster and larger than ever"), and stock workshop photos that prove nothing about the work itself.
- **Language:** English only.
- **Dark mode:** none found (81 KB of CSS checked).
- **Type and colour:** Gilroy headings with Adelle Sans body. A warm editorial look.

### 37signals: https://37signals.com
- **Job:** shows fit through values. There is no hero. The homepage is a numbered index, 00–37, of short principles: "Planning is guessing", "Meetings aren't free", "Work isn't war", "Context > consistency".
- **Mechanic worth stealing:** **principles as positioning.** A short numbered list of how you work tells a client more about fit than any services grid does. For Jarkko it could be five or six principles: how he scopes, how often he ships, how he uses AI, why one person.
- **Reject:** no services, no contact path. It works for a product company, but would sink a consultancy.
- **Language:** English only.
- **Dark mode:** none found.
- **Type and colour:** Lab Grotesque plus Lab Grotesque Mono, monochrome. Typographic minimalism.

### Panic: https://panic.com
- **Job:** shows craft and personality. The H1 is "We code apps, publish games, and make Playdate". Each product block has a name, a one-line pitch with wit ("a lovely morning in the village, and you are a horrible goose"), its platforms, and a buy link.
- **Mechanic worth stealing:** **the one-sentence company definition with three verbs.** It gives the whole range of a small company in one breath. Jarkko's version might be "I build web apps, ship iOS/macOS apps, and coach volleyball", which makes the range read as a strength.
- **Reject:** not built for hiring. There is no services or contact flow for clients.
- **Language:** a Japanese site in the footer, with `hreflang="ja"` declared. A clean pattern: a separate localised site, linked from the footer, with correct hreflang.
- **Dark mode:** none found.
- **Type and colour:** Hellix, Roobert and Booton with pixel-art sprites. Playful and colourful.

### Futurice: https://www.futurice.com (Finnish, about 1,000 people)
- **Job:** proves competence at scale. The H1 is "Digital transformation with measurable outcomes". Four results with numbers sit in the hero area (K-Ruoka 1.5M+ installs, VR -60% cancellations, FC Bayern Basketball app rated 4.9/5, EnBW 20% faster retrieval).
- **Mechanic worth stealing:** **a strip of numeric proof directly under the hero**: four short "client + one number" items. Nothing is easier to scan.
- **Reject:** half-translated Finnish pages. `/fi` has a translated H1 and nav, but English case-study headings and slugs, English blog titles and an English footer. A bilingual site that switches language halfway down the page tells a Finnish reader they are second-class.
- **Language:** a "Valitse kieli" disclosure listing English, Suomi, Deutsch and Svenska in their own names, with path prefixes (`/fi/`).
- **Dark mode:** one `prefers-color-scheme` reference. Not confirmed.
- **Type and colour:** not verified.

### Reaktor: https://www.reaktor.com (Finnish, large)
- **Job:** proves prestige. "Hello!", then "We're a global technology company that designs and builds category-defining digital solutions". The logo wall includes the UN, Netflix, Airbus and Finnair.
- **Mechanic worth stealing:** little for a solo consultant. At most, the "Hello!" greeting before the claim shows a human voice can sit on top of a corporate claim.
- **Reject:** adjectives standing in for evidence ("category-defining", "a Culture of Better").
- **Language:** `hreflang` for en and ja-jp. No Finnish found on the homepage, which is notable for a Finnish company.
- **Dark mode:** none found. **Type and colour:** not verified.

### Brad Frost: https://bradfrost.com, and Sara Soueidan: https://www.sarasoueidan.com (solo voices, for contrast)
- **Job:** voice and authority, not hiring. Brad's H1 is "Hey there! I'm Brad Frost." His title tag lists "Creator, Designer, Teacher, Speaker, Writer, Musician, Artist". Sara's is "Hi, I'm Sara." Neither homepage offers services or a contact email. Sara has only social links.
- **Mechanic worth stealing:** Brad shows the whole person openly. Music and art sit in the nav as peers of the courses, which makes the "volleyball coach" side legitimate rather than embarrassing. Sara has a note that the content was written without LLMs, which is a credible stance in 2026.
- **Reject:** both bury the hiring path. "Hey there!" works for an educator but wastes the first 10 seconds for a buyer.
- **Language:** English only. **Dark mode:** none found on Brad's site. Sara's was not checked. Both sites were mid-redesign when fetched.
- **Type and colour:** Brad uses League Gothic and Montserrat, loud and poster-like.

## 2. Case-study pages: the three best

| | CSS Wizardry: ISO.org | thoughtbot: CloseKnit Health | Clearleft: Virgin Holidays |
|---|---|---|---|
| URL | https://csswizardry.com/case-studies/iso-org/ | https://thoughtbot.com/case-studies/closeknit-health | https://clearleft.com/work/virgin-holidays |
| Opens with | Voice: "ISO is the standard in standards." | Client need, plus a header strip listing client and services/stack | The story framing, then three engagement goals |
| Proof | One CrUX chart; before/after numbers with % change; admits INP didn't move | One headline metric (10x release speed); 3 named client quotes; product mockups; video | Results block (3 outcomes); +8% web sales over 2 yrs; team doubled; 18 months |
| Structure | Prose: brief, approach, results, handover (Trello backlog + exec summary) | Result **before** What we did; question headings | Results, then Full Story under question headings |
| Length | ~500 words | Medium | ~1,100 words |
| CTA | "If you need some of the same, get in touch" + Book a Free Call + Next case | "What does success look like for your team?" Let's talk | "You're unique: Email Rich" |

**What to combine for koivupiilo.fi:**
1. A header strip with client, year, role, stack and duration.
2. A one-sentence problem in the client's language.
3. The result as a number, or as a concrete before and after.
4. Two or three short sections headed by the client's question.
5. One real artefact: a screenshot, chart or code excerpt.
6. Optionally, a named quote.
7. What was handed over (team, docs, backlog).
8. "If you need some of the same" with a direct email.

Keep it to 400–700 words.

## 3. Cross-cutting observations

- **Dark mode:** none of the fetched sites clearly honours `prefers-color-scheme`. Following the system appearance properly, with no toggle, is an easy way to stand out.
- **Bilingual:** no site in the set does FI/EN well. Kisko offers a single English summary page. Futurice translates only part of each page. Reaktor has no Finnish at all. Panic's separate locale site with `hreflang` is the cleanest pattern. **Full parity in both languages, with translated slugs and hreflang pairs, would beat every Finnish studio in this set.**
- **Contact:** the sites that are best for clients put **a named human** behind the CTA (Clearleft's "Email Rich", Kisko's named booking links). No site gives "what to include" guidance or promises a response time. That gap is ours to take.
- **Pricing:** no site states a price. Kisko comes closest with its fixed monthly Durability fee and its cost calculator. A price floor or typical range for a solo consultant would beat everyone here.

## 4. Synthesis: "ours must beat X at Y"

### (a) First 10 seconds: "is this the right person?"
- Ours must beat **CSS Wizardry** at **niche clarity in the H1**, but for a broader brief. One line should name the kind of client and problem ("senior product engineer for small teams shipping web and Apple apps"), with a real photo and "one person, 30 years" stated as the advantage, not hidden.
- Ours must beat **Panic** at **the three-verb self-definition**. Rails/Elixir web, SwiftUI apps and ML should fit into one memorable sentence, with the Rails-book credential as the proof that follows, not a logo wall Jarkko may not have.

### (b) Services: "can they do my thing, and how is it engaged?"
- Ours must beat **Kisko Labs** at **stage-based offers with a price signal**. That means three plain-named engagements (for example a fixed-price scoping sprint, a build, and a monthly retainer), each with duration, deliverable, and a "from €X" floor or typical range. No ™ and no calculator needed.
- Ours must beat **Set Studio** at **turning "solo" into the reason to hire**. Use a short "working with me vs. an agency" comparison: you talk to the person writing the code, no handoffs, one invoice, and an honest line about capacity (one or two clients at a time).

### (c) Case study: proof
- Ours must beat **thoughtbot** at **outcome-first case studies**. Each one needs a number or a concrete before/after in the headline and a header strip (client, year, role, stack, duration), but tighter: under 700 words, with a real screenshot or code excerpt instead of stock photography.
- Ours must beat **CSS Wizardry** at **candour plus artefacts**. Keep Harry's honesty about what didn't move, add a named client quote (which his case study lacks), and show what was handed over. Every case study must exist in full in both FI and EN.

### (d) Contact: low-friction first step
- Ours must beat **Clearleft** at **a named human behind the CTA**. A visible email address and a calendar link for a 20–30 minute call should sit on every page, plus the three things to include that Clearleft omits: what you're building, where it's stuck, and the timeline or budget range.
- Ours must beat **CSS Wizardry / thoughtbot** at **setting expectations**. State the response time ("reply within one working day"), current availability ("taking new work from <month>"), and what happens on the first call. Do not use a multi-step form.
