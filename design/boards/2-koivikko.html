Concept 2 · Koivikko (the grove)

Koivupiilo

The place leads. Your grove, in this season’s photo, opens the site, set in a calm editorial serif. The birch is shown, not drawn. It reads as a careful Finnish company with somewhere real behind it.

Why it fits the job

A club chair or a treasurer gets warmth and a sense of place before they read a word, and the facts are still a scroll away, in the same register strip as concept 1.

The photo changes with the season in Kangasala (positioning rule 3): it’s one quiet, ownable detail, and visitors who come back notice it.

  • Place as proof. “Koivupiilo, Kangasala” is visible, not just stated in the footer.
  • Serif for the company’s voice, grotesk for facts and UI. The split makes the third-person voice (D16) feel composed, not corporate.
  • Depends on photos. It needs four approved photos of the same grove, one per season, with no house and no people.

The bark element: the photo is the pattern

Studio

Every inner page opens with a narrow crop of one trunk from the grove beside its heading. It’s a different trunk on each page, so the site is literally a walk through the same grove.

There’s no drawn pattern. The trunk shows up as photography only: the full grove on the front page, single trunks as page spines, and the bark itself, full-bleed, behind the 404.

That needs six trunk crops from your library (same grove, any year). The trunk on the left is the one we have (2013).

The season

The photo is picked at build time from the month in Kangasala. It needs no script, and a rebuild on the first of each season is enough. Every photo shows the same viewpoint if possible, with no house, no children and no coordinates in the published file.

Motion

  • The photo arrives. The front-page photo fades in from the paper colour: 600 ms, ease-out. Purpose: no hard pop on slow connections. It doesn’t move after that.
  • Links: underline thickness 1 → 2 px, 120 ms.
  • Nothing else. No scroll reveals, no parallax on the photo (tempting, and banned), no autoplaying seasons.
  • Reduced motion: the photo is simply there.

References, at the bar

  • Koto: warmth carried by one real thing and generous space. We take the restraint around the photo.
  • Werklig: exactness in the details. The register strip, the list rules and the type scale are as strict as in concept 1.
  • Panic: the one sentence. Here it’s in a serif, beside the grove.

Weak at

  • Photo-dependent. It needs four season photos and six trunks, all approved, before launch. Until then it’s half a concept.
  • No mark. The wordmark is just set type. Product footers and the App Store get a name, not a symbol.
  • Closer to “lifestyle”. A photo-led serif site can read as a cottage or a café. The register strip and the work list have to hold it to “company”.
